    <h2 class="section-h reveal">The <em>4 Ps</em>, positioning in operational reality.</h2>
    <p class="section-p reveal">Positioning must translate into operational reality. The four levers that ship B2B messaging into campaigns, sales decks, and partner packs.</p>

    <div class="four-ps-wrap reveal">
      <div class="ps-quad">
        <div class="ps-label">Price</div>
        <div class="ps-title">Price</div>
        <div class="ps-body">How you charge must match how buyers perceive value. Outcomes-based pricing signals confidence in your methodology. If your price structure doesn't reflect your PMF positioning, it undermines it.</div>
        <div class="ps-letter">P</div>
      </div>
      <div class="ps-quad">
        <div class="ps-label">Place</div>
        <div class="ps-title">Place</div>
        <div class="ps-body">Where and how you reach buyers. For B2B tech, this means the right channels (LinkedIn, partner events, SDR outreach) not the broadest ones. Channel selection follows the buyer's journey, not convenience.</div>
        <div class="ps-letter">P</div>
      </div>
      <div class="ps-quad">
        <div class="ps-label">Product</div>
        <div class="ps-title">Product</div>
        <div class="ps-body">Not the features list: the story of what the product does for the buyer. Positioning defines which capabilities matter most, to which segment, in which words. The product message is the PMF in action.</div>
        <div class="ps-letter">P</div>
      </div>
      <div class="ps-quad">
        <div class="ps-label">Promotion</div>
        <div class="ps-title">Promotion</div>
        <div class="ps-body">Campaigns, content, SDR follow-up, partner enablement. Every promotional asset speaks from the same PMF. Consistency is what makes the message land, and repeat inside the sales conversation.</div>
        <div class="ps-letter">P</div>
      </div>
